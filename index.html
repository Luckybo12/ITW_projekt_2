Hi, I'm Bing Bong.

The cheerful climber. 22 years old. Friend to every peak.

I grew up in a tiny mountain town called Bellrock Village, learned to climb before I learned long division, and I've been ringing my grandfather's bronze bell at the foot of every summit ever since. This little site is my way of waving hello to the world. Pull up a chair, the kettle is on.

02 — About

A little about me

Three short answers to the questions people usually ask me when I sit down by their campfire.

2.1 Background

I was born in Bellrock Village, a one-road climbing town tucked into the Cascadia Highlands. My grandfather, Old Man Borrow, ran the only gear shop in the valley and put me on a rope when I was eight. He said a child who can climb a tree can climb a mountain — he was mostly right, give or take a few scraped knees.

At fifteen I got lost in a snowstorm on Mt. Whisperpine. The local search team found me eight hours later, mostly cold and entirely embarrassed. The leader of that team handed me a thermos and told me, "Next time, you're carrying it." I joined the Peak Scouts Guild three years later and now I lead expeditions for first-time climbers — usually the nervous ones.

2.2 Personality

If you've met me, you probably heard me before you saw me. I ring a small bronze bell at the start of every climb — a habit grandfather taught me when I was small. He said the mountain should know you're coming, and that it's only polite. I have never argued with him about it.

  • Cheerful — even when the weather is not.
  • Friendly — I will introduce myself to anyone within ten metres.
  • A little clumsy — but I always land on my feet, eventually.
  • Patient — mountains take their time, and so do I.

2.3 Skills

Things I can do, more or less reliably:

  • Alpine climbing
  • Cold-weather survival
  • Map & star navigation
  • Campfire cooking
  • Languages (EN, CZ, FR, a little DE)

03 — Adventures

Climbs I'm proud of

A small handful of expeditions that taught me something. There are a lot more that mostly taught me to bring an extra pair of socks.

  1. 2018

    Mt. Whisperpine — first solo summit

    My first real climb without grandfather on the other end of the rope. Three days, two snowstorms, one cracked thermos. I rang the bell at the top and shouted his name. I'd like to think he heard.

  2. 2021

    The Razor Ridge expedition

    Ten days, four climbers, one impossible-looking ridgeline. We had to wait out a blizzard for thirty hours in a tent meant for twenty. We came back friends, which is the best thing a mountain can do.

  3. 2022

    Glacier Crown traverse — winter

    A four-peak winter traverse with the Guild. I broke a crampon on day two and finished the last summit with a borrowed pair two sizes too big. It still counts.

  4. 2024

    Twin Spires — winter ascent

    Both peaks in a single push, in February. Cold enough that my bell froze to my glove. I rang it twice on the way down anyway. The mountain didn't seem to mind.

  5. 2026

    The Forbidden Peak — coming soon

    Currently planning. It has been climbed once, by a team of seven, in 1979. Grandfather was on that team. I'd like to go and say hello for him.

04 — Gear

What I carry up the mountain

Five things I never leave the valley without. Half are practical, half are just sentimental, and to be honest I cannot always tell which is which.

Bronze bell

Heirloom from grandfather. Rung at the start of every climb. Slightly dented from the Razor Ridge expedition. Still works perfectly.

Heirloom ice axe

Wooden shaft, hand-forged head. Older than I am. Has a name carved into the side that I will not share, because grandfather wouldn't like it if I did.

Broken-in boots

Three winters old, properly waterproof, and they finally stopped giving me blisters last spring. I will wear these until the soles fall off and probably one climb past that.

Four-season tent

Light enough to carry, sturdy enough to sleep in during a blizzard. Comes with a small patch on the rain-fly, which is a story for around the campfire.

Field journal

Notes, weather sketches, and unflattering doodles of my climbing partners. I have filled seven of these so far. The eighth is currently on chapter three.
